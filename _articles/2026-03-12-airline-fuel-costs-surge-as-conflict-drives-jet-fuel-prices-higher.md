Airlines around the world are adjusting ticket prices and fuel surcharges as jet fuel costs climb sharply amid ongoing conflict in the Middle East.

Industry analysts report that jet fuel prices have surged significantly since military operations targeting Iran began in late February.

The increase has forced several carriers to review fare structures on both domestic and international routes.

Some airlines have introduced temporary fuel surcharges, while others have raised base ticket prices to offset the higher operating costs.

Long-haul routes between Europe, Asia, and Oceania have been particularly affected, as aircraft are also being rerouted to avoid restricted airspace across parts of the Middle East.

Aviation industry groups warn that continued volatility in oil markets could further pressure airline profitability in the months ahead.

Carriers with extensive fuel hedging contracts may be partially insulated from the immediate impact, though analysts say prolonged disruptions could still affect financial outlooks.

Travel demand remains steady so far, but experts say sustained price increases could eventually reduce passenger volumes if fares continue rising.
