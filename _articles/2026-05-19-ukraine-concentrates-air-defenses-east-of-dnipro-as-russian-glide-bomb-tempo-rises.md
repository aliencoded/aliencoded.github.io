KYIV — Ukraine has shifted air-defense assets east of the Dnipro River to address a rising Russian glide-bomb tempo against the new fortification line west of Pokrovsk, Ukrainian military officials confirmed Tuesday morning. The redeployment, executed over the past five days, has moved approximately a battalion's worth of Patriot, IRIS-T, and NASAMS launchers from rear-area positions into forward sites covering the principal Donetsk-axis defensive belt.

The shift reflects what Ukrainian air-defense commanders have characterized as a meaningful acceleration in Russian glide-bomb sorties against the new fortification line over the past week. A Ukrainian general staff spokesperson, in a Tuesday-morning briefing, said Russian aircraft had conducted approximately one hundred forty glide-bomb sorties against forward Ukrainian positions during the past seven days, an increase of roughly thirty-five percent over the prior-week tempo. The bombs, most of them KAB-500 and KAB-1500 variants with extended-range guidance kits, are launched from Russian aircraft operating well behind the front line and have become one of the more persistent threats to the fortification belt.

The forward redeployment carries operational risk. Patriot and IRIS-T launchers placed within range of Russian short-range ballistic missiles and one-way attack drones become themselves higher-value targets, and the move thins Ukraine's rear-area air-defense coverage for cities and critical infrastructure further west. A senior Ukrainian air-defense officer, in a background briefing, said the redeployment had been authorized by Ukrainian command "with full awareness of the trade-off" and that the rear-area gap would be addressed through the air-defense surge European partners had committed at the Ramstein contact group meeting on May 8.

The European air-defense delivery schedule has been a focus of Ukrainian planning since the Ramstein commitments. Approximately twenty additional Patriot launchers and fourteen IRIS-T launchers are scheduled to arrive in Ukraine through the second quarter, with the bulk of deliveries scheduled for the back half of June. A senior European defense official, contacted Tuesday morning, said the delivery schedule remained on track and that the first additional Patriot battery would reach a Ukrainian receiving site by the end of the week.

The Russian glide-bomb tempo's acceleration has paralleled the broader pattern of Russian reconnaissance-in-force probes against the new fortification line that began Friday. Open-source military analysts who reviewed Russian and Ukrainian Telegram channels Tuesday morning identified at least nine separate Russian probing actions against the line during the past four days, with the actions concentrated against the northern and central sectors. Ukrainian defenders have engaged the probes consistently short of the line.

A senior Ukrainian general staff officer, in a Tuesday-morning briefing, said the combined pattern of glide-bomb tempo and reconnaissance probes was consistent with the general staff's working assessment that Russian forces were preparing for a larger offensive operation against the fortification line in the late-June to mid-July window. The officer said the Ukrainian objective through the intervening period would be to preserve the line's integrity while imposing the maximum sustainable cost on Russian forces conducting the preparatory operations.

Russian commentary on the air-defense redeployment, published in Telegram channels Tuesday morning, characterized the move as evidence that Ukrainian forces had recognized the threat the glide-bomb campaign was presenting to the fortification belt. One widely followed Russian military commentator wrote that the redeployment "creates new opportunities and new vulnerabilities for Ukrainian forces in roughly equal measure."

The U.S. defense secretary's office, in a Tuesday-morning statement, said the United States was "in continuous coordination" with Ukrainian counterparts on the air-defense disposition and indicated that the U.S. munitions package included in the war supplemental signed Friday morning included substantial Patriot interceptor deliveries on a timeline calibrated to the rising threat tempo. The first wave of interceptors from the supplemental is expected to reach Ukrainian receiving sites by mid-June.

The EU windfall package, council adoption of which is scheduled for May 27, will provide additional resources for Ukrainian air-defense procurement under the framework's defense-procurement stream. The Commission's published mechanics Monday allocated approximately thirty-five percent of the windfall proceeds to defense procurement under a procurement-only restriction, with air-defense the principal category. A senior Ukrainian defense-procurement official, in a Tuesday-morning briefing, said the windfall mechanism would substantially augment Ukraine's air-defense procurement capacity beginning in the third quarter.

The Pokrovsk operational situation itself remained relatively static through the past forty-eight hours, with Russian forces continuing urban-clearance operations in the city's western districts and consolidating their hold on the broader salient. The Ukrainian general staff said the principal Russian operational priority across the broader Donetsk axis remained the buildup of supplies and forces for the anticipated offensive cycle rather than immediate territorial advance.

A senior Ukrainian regional administration official, in a Tuesday-morning briefing from Dnipro, said civilian evacuations from settlements within the fortification belt's immediate operational area had been substantially completed during the past three weeks, with approximately twelve thousand residents having been moved to receiving facilities in central and western Ukraine. The official said the evacuation had been conducted on a structured basis and that the receiving facilities had adequate capacity for additional evacuations if the operational situation required.

Air-raid alerts across Ukrainian eastern and southern oblasts have continued at elevated frequency over the past week, with the principal Russian missile and drone activity concentrated on infrastructure targets rather than on the immediate fortification belt. A senior Ukrainian energy-grid official, contacted Tuesday morning, said the grid's operational resilience had been maintained through the recent attack pattern and that the broader pre-winter resilience program was proceeding on schedule.

The Ramstein contact group's next scheduled meeting is set for June 12 in Brussels, with the substantive agenda expected to include a review of the air-defense delivery progress, the early operational experience of the windfall mechanism's defense-procurement stream, and the broader strategic posture for Ukrainian operations through the second half of 2026.
