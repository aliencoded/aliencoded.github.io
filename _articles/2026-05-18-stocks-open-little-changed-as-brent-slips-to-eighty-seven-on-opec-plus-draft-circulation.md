NEW YORK — U.S. equity benchmarks opened little changed Monday morning as Brent crude slipped below $87 per barrel on draft circulation of OPEC-plus production proposals ahead of the cartel's June 1 Vienna meeting, with the early-session action reflecting a mix of soft data signals from the morning's regional manufacturing print and modestly easing energy-sector positioning.

The S&P 500 opened at 5,847.32, approximately seven-tenths of a point above Friday's close, with the broad index trading in a narrow range through the opening hour. The Nasdaq Composite opened modestly higher, supported by the Salesforce-Anthropic integration announcement's positive equity response. The Dow Jones Industrial Average opened approximately twenty points below Friday's close, with industrial-sector positioning reflecting the morning's Empire manufacturing print.

Brent crude opened at $87.42 per barrel, approximately seventy-two cents below Friday's $88.14 settlement, with the principal contract trading down through the opening hour to a session low of $86.94 before stabilizing. The decline reflected the cautious draft circulation of OPEC-plus production proposals that several producer-group officials had communicated through Saturday and Sunday channels, with the content suggesting that the cartel was contemplating modestly tighter production-restraint provisions for the second half of 2026.

A senior Saudi energy-ministry official, in remarks delivered Sunday afternoon at a regional energy forum, said the cartel's cautious approach to the June meeting reflected "the realities of the current price environment" and indicated that the producer-group framework would be "calibrated" to operational conditions through the second half of the year. Those remarks were responsible for the early Brent-market positioning.

The yield on the ten-year Treasury note traded at four-point-three-six percent in the opening hour, two basis points below Friday's closing level, with the softer Empire manufacturing print supporting modest demand for duration. The two-year note traded at four-point-five-eight percent, essentially unchanged on the session, with front-end positioning reflecting an unusually heavy Federal Reserve speaker calendar through the week.

The dollar index gained approximately one-tenth of a percent against a broad basket of major currencies, with the euro essentially unchanged at $1.0852 and the yen trading at 148.92 per dollar. The yen's strength against the dollar through the past several weeks has reflected the widening Bank of Japan policy-normalization trajectory.

The Salesforce-Anthropic integration announcement provided the principal individual-equity story of the opening session. Salesforce shares opened approximately three-and-a-half percent above Friday's close and traded up further through the opening hour, with the equity's positive response reflecting market expectations of accelerated enterprise-customer growth through the integration framework. The announcement's sector-level implications drove positive performance across the broader enterprise-software complex.

A senior equity strategist at a major U.S. brokerage, in a Monday-morning client note, said market positioning entering the week was "constructive but cautious," reflecting the favorable data flow over the past two weeks while acknowledging the important macro events scheduled for the coming days. The strategist noted that the S&P 500's proximity to its all-time February high had contributed to more measured risk-taking entering the week.

The earnings calendar for the week is light, with the principal releases concentrated in the large-retailer complex. Walmart reports Tuesday before the open, Target reports Wednesday before the open, and TJX reports Thursday before the open. Consensus expectations for the three retailers' results have been elevated through the past two weeks following Friday's strong April retail-sales print.

A senior retail-sector analyst at a major U.S. brokerage, in a Monday-morning client note, said the consensus framework for the three retailers' results had been informed by Friday's strong April data but cautioned that the elevated consensus could create downside risk if specific-retailer results underperform the bar.

Energy-sector positioning is expected to track the trajectory of OPEC-plus pre-meeting communications through the week. Tuesday's monthly OPEC oil-market report and Wednesday morning's Bloomberg interview with the Saudi energy minister will be the two most closely watched scheduled events.

The Aramco institutional-investor roadshow continues through the week, with Hong Kong events Monday morning, Tokyo Wednesday morning, and Seoul Friday morning. The roadshow's content has been closely watched by sector analysts since Sunday's Singapore opening.

The Federal Reserve speaker calendar begins with Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack's Monday-morning remarks at a Cleveland community-banking conference, with the most important event being Vice Chair Philip Jefferson's Wednesday 12:30 p.m. Eastern speech at the New York Economic Club. Five FOMC participants in total are scheduled to speak through the week.

Tuesday's housing-starts data will provide the first significant data point of the week, with consensus expecting a modestly softer print relative to the March result. Existing home sales follow Thursday, and the University of Michigan's preliminary May consumer-sentiment survey closes the week Friday morning.

The week's tone will be established by the combination of the morning Empire print, Tuesday's housing data, Wednesday's vice-chair speech, and the OPEC-plus pre-meeting communications. The S&P 500 enters the week within one percent of its February all-time high, with positioning that several strategists have characterized as approaching but not yet reaching the calibrated-caution threshold typical of late-cycle market environments.
