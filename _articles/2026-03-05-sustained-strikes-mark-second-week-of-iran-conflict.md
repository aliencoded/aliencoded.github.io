Military operations connected to the Iran conflict entered a second week of sustained activity, with defense analysts describing a pattern of continued strikes rather than the brief escalation many observers had anticipated.

Officials familiar with the situation say strike tempo has remained steady across multiple theaters, including missile and drone activity directed at military and infrastructure targets. Regional governments have responded by maintaining elevated alert levels.

Independent analysts note that the prolonged pace of operations has begun to shift assumptions among diplomatic and economic actors who initially treated the conflict as a short-duration episode. Insurance markets, shipping operators, and airline route planners are now recalculating exposure on longer time horizons.

Defense ministries in several neighboring states have signaled that current deployments will continue indefinitely. Spokespeople have framed the posture as defensive, but reservist call-ups and accelerated procurement of interceptor stocks suggest planning for an extended scenario.

Diplomatic channels remain active, with multiple third-party governments offering to facilitate communication between the parties. Analysts caution that the absence of any visible off-ramp has narrowed the space for short-term de-escalation.

Observers expect the second week to be defined less by single dramatic events than by the cumulative effect of sustained operations on regional logistics, civilian movement, and financial markets. A widening circle of secondary actors is being drawn into contingency planning.

The longer the current tempo holds, analysts say, the more permanent the regional realignments around it are likely to become.
