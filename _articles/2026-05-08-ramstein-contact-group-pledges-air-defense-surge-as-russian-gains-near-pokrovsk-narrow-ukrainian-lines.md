<p>
European defense ministers and a thinner-than-usual American delegation closed a Ramstein-format meeting on Friday with the largest single tranche of air-defense pledges to Ukraine since the Iran war began, committing roughly 7.2 billion euros in interceptors, batteries, artillery shells and electronic-warfare kit over the next six months as Russian forces narrowed the contested approach to Pokrovsk to its closest point in fourteen months of fighting.
</p>

<p>
The package, announced by German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius alongside Ukrainian Defense Minister Rustem Umerov, includes a German-financed acceleration of IRIS-T deliveries, a Dutch-Czech-Danish artillery shell push aiming to land 420,000 rounds by November, and a U.K.-led pool to fund Patriot interceptor production runs through 2027. France committed an additional Mamba/SAMP-T battery and a tranche of AASM-guided bombs. Italy added a CAMM-ER short-range battery. The Nordics and Baltics, pooling under a Stockholm-led mechanism, pledged 1.1 billion euros for drone production inside Ukraine.
</p>

<p>
Conspicuously, no new U.S. weapons package was unveiled. Acting Under Secretary of Defense for Policy Mark Reilly, who led the American delegation in place of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, told reporters Washington would "continue existing presidential drawdown authority shipments" but said any standalone Ukraine supplemental remained tied to negotiations over the Iran reconstruction bill still moving through Congress.
</p>

<p>
"Europe is carrying the near-term load, and Europe is prepared to carry it," Pistorius said in his closing statement. "But we are clear-eyed that the volumes Ukraine needs to hold its lines through summer require American industrial capacity. The contact group is a partnership, not a substitute."
</p>

<h2>Pokrovsk creeps closer</h2>

<p>
The ministers met as Russian forces continued a slow but unmistakable advance toward Pokrovsk, the Donetsk region logistics hub whose loss would force Ukraine to reorganize supply lines for a 60-kilometer arc of front. Ukrainian General Staff figures issued Friday morning placed Russian positions inside the village of Novoekonomichne, roughly four kilometers from the eastern outskirts of Pokrovsk, with separate axes pressing toward Kostiantynivka and Chasiv Yar.
</p>

<p>
The Ukrainian command reported 207 combat engagements in the previous 24 hours, the highest one-day count since mid-February. Glide-bomb strikes, which Ukrainian officials say have been the single most punishing weapon of the spring offensive, totaled 152 in the same period, concentrated on rear logistics nodes around Dobropillia and Druzhkivka.
</p>

<p>
"Moscow is buying square kilometers with extraordinary casualty rates, and that has been the pattern for months," said Hanna Shelest, a Kyiv-based security analyst with the Foreign Policy Council. "What has changed in April and May is the cumulative weight. Ukrainian air defense ran thin during the Iran war, and the Russians are running their offensive into that thinness. The Ramstein package, if it lands on schedule, addresses the air-defense gap. It does not on its own change the manpower arithmetic on the ground."
</p>

<p>
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy, addressing the Ramstein gathering by secure video link, said Russian forces had launched 1,840 glide bombs at Ukrainian positions and rear areas in April alone, a figure he called "industrial-scale terror against Ukrainian cities and supply lines." He named Pokrovsk, Kupiansk and Sumy as the three areas where Ukrainian commanders had requested priority deliveries of medium-range systems.
</p>

<h2>Patriot pipeline and the production problem</h2>

<p>
The most closely watched portion of Friday's announcement was a British-led mechanism to forward-purchase Patriot interceptor production slots from Raytheon and its European partners, with funding pooled from the U.K., Norway, the Netherlands and Germany. The arrangement, officials said, would lock in roughly 540 PAC-3 MSE interceptors for Ukrainian use through 2027, in addition to existing U.S.-funded production.
</p>

<p>
Patriot interceptors, used during the Iran war to defend Saudi and Emirati facilities and U.S. bases in Iraq, became scarce in March and April, and several European officials privately concede that diversions during the Gulf crisis left Ukrainian batteries operating below sustainable rates by late April. Pistorius declined to detail diversion figures but said the German government was "rebuilding Ukrainian stockpiles in full" and would accelerate two IRIS-T units originally scheduled for delivery in the third quarter.
</p>

<p>
"The bottleneck is no longer money," said John Reilly, a London-based analyst at Citi who tracks defense industrial output. "It is interceptor production lines and propellant. Friday's announcement is a credible attempt to forward-fund the lines, but even with cash on the table you do not double Patriot output in twelve months."
</p>

<h2>Washington's awkward posture</h2>

<p>
The American absence at the top of the Ramstein guest list was the diplomatic subtext of the meeting. Hegseth had been expected to attend but canceled Thursday evening, citing a Senate Armed Services hearing on the Pentagon's role during the Iran war. Senate Foreign Relations Committee ranking member Sen. Jeanne Shaheen, in a statement Friday, called the cancellation "an unforced error at a moment when allies need to see American resolve on Ukraine, not American distraction."
</p>

<p>
A senior White House official, speaking on condition of anonymity to describe internal deliberations, said the administration's position remained that any supplemental Ukraine package would move alongside the Iran reconstruction bill, with both expected on the Senate floor by the end of May. The official said President Donald Trump had not personally opposed a stand-alone Ukraine vehicle but viewed the combined package as "the most efficient path."
</p>

<h2>Moscow's read</h2>

<p>
Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova dismissed the Ramstein announcement as "another European theater of self-congratulation that will not arrive in time to matter." Russian state television led its evening bulletins with coverage of Victory Day rehearsals in Moscow and footage of advances near Pokrovsk, with comparatively little airtime devoted to the Ramstein commitments.
</p>

<p>
Independent Russian military commentators outside the country, several of whom monitor open-source battlefield data, said the Kremlin appeared to be racing to consolidate the Pokrovsk corridor before European air defense deliveries reach the front in volume. "The Russian planning horizon for this phase is roughly through July," said one analyst, who asked not to be named because of risks to family members inside Russia. "After that, the math gets harder for them."
</p>

<p>
NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte, who attended the Ramstein session, said the alliance's military committee would convene a special session next week to align force-posture decisions in eastern Europe with the new Ukraine commitments. He said additional steps on Black Sea maritime patrols and Baltic air policing would be announced following that meeting.
</p>
