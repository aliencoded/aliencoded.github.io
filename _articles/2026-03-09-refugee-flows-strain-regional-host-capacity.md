Cross-border movement of civilians seeking safety from the Iran conflict is straining host capacity in several neighboring states as the conflict enters its second full week, international relief agencies said Monday.

Movements have been concentrated along multiple border zones, with families seeking temporary shelter in towns and informal settlements just inside neighboring territories. Numbers remain difficult to verify in real time, but agencies say the trajectory has been steadily upward over the past several days.

Host governments have responded with a mix of formal reception arrangements and case-by-case accommodations. Several have temporarily expanded visa-on-arrival provisions or extended documentation timelines to allow movement to proceed without administrative bottlenecks at the border itself.

Shelter and food assistance have been the most acute immediate needs. Aid agencies are working through existing logistics partners to pre-position supplies along likely movement corridors. Medical screening has been added at several crossing points, with attention to communicable disease risk and trauma care.

Receiving communities, many already absorbing populations from earlier displacement episodes, have raised concerns about housing, water, and schooling capacity. Local authorities have pressed national governments and international agencies for additional resources to support both incoming families and host communities.

International funding commitments have begun to arrive, though agencies note that pledges typically lag actual disbursement by weeks. Bridge financing mechanisms have been activated in some cases to allow operations to proceed ahead of formal contributions.

Onward movement patterns remain uncertain. Some families have indicated intent to return when conditions permit, while others are exploring options further afield. Agencies caution that displacement patterns often extend beyond the duration of underlying conflicts.

Protection concerns include the risk of exploitation, family separation, and difficulties accessing essential services. Specialized agencies have deployed staff focused on child protection, gender-based violence prevention, and support for individuals with medical or psychological needs.

The next several weeks will be critical in determining whether the response can keep pace with movement patterns. International agencies have emphasized that early investment in host community support is often the most cost-effective way to sustain protection over the longer term.
