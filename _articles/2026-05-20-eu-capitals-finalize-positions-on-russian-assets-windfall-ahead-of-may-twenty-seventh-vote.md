BRUSSELS — European Union member states finalized their national positions on the Russian-assets windfall package Wednesday afternoon, with twenty-five of the bloc's twenty-seven member capitals communicating affirmative voting positions to the Council Secretariat ahead of next Tuesday's formal adoption vote. Hungary and Slovakia, both of which had previewed their positions earlier in the week, confirmed they would abstain rather than vote affirmatively.

The voting pattern, while not surprising given the diplomatic signals through the past two weeks, was the first formal confirmation that the package would clear the Council's qualified-majority voting threshold without procedural difficulty. The threshold requires affirmative votes from at least fifty-five percent of member states representing at least sixty-five percent of the EU population. The twenty-five affirmative votes substantially exceed both thresholds.

A senior Council Secretariat official, in a Wednesday-afternoon Brussels briefing, said the formal position confirmations had been received from twenty-three member capitals during the past forty-eight hours, with two final positions filed Wednesday morning ahead of the Council Secretariat's noon cutoff for Tuesday's vote preparation. The official said the substantive content of the affirmative positions reflected what he characterized as "the kind of cross-bloc alignment we have not seen on a Russia-related question in this Council in eighteen months."

Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever, whose government has carried the principal drafting role given the Euroclear custody of the underlying frozen assets, said in a Wednesday-afternoon Brussels statement that the package's legal foundation had been "stress-tested" through the diplomatic process and that the Belgian government remained "fully confident" in the framework's resilience to subsequent legal challenge. De Wever credited the Commission Legal Service's work and the European Court of Justice's early-May preliminary ruling as the foundations of that confidence.

The Hungarian and Slovak abstention positions had been previewed by both governments earlier in the week but were formalized Wednesday in communications to the Council Secretariat. Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán's Wednesday-afternoon Budapest statement said Hungary had "maintained its consistent reservations" but had concluded that "formal opposition was not warranted given the framework's procedural integrity." Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico's parallel statement was substantively similar.

Both abstaining governments confirmed that they would participate in the framework's subsequent operational implementation despite the abstention positions on Tuesday's vote. A senior Hungarian foreign-ministry official, contacted Wednesday afternoon for background, said Hungary would "cooperate within the framework's operational requirements" while preserving Hungary's broader policy reservations about the framework's substantive direction.

The substantive content of Tuesday's Council action will include both the formal adoption of the framework's legal text and the activation of the operational provisions covering the first quarterly disbursement, scheduled for early July. The Commission's published mechanics from Monday remain unmodified, with the three principal allocation streams — Ukrainian budgetary support at forty percent, defense procurement at thirty-five percent, and infrastructure reconstruction at twenty-five percent — set to begin disbursing under the framework's standard procedures following Tuesday's adoption.

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiha, in a Wednesday-afternoon Kyiv briefing, said Ukraine had been "in continuous coordination" with the Commission and member-state counterparts through the position-finalization period and that Ukraine's preparation for the first quarterly disbursement was "essentially complete." Sybiha noted that the substantive Ukrainian operational planning had been calibrated to permit immediate deployment of disbursed funds across the three allocation streams.

A senior Ukrainian defense-procurement official, contacted Wednesday afternoon, said the Ukrainian defense-procurement framework was prepared to direct the first defense-procurement-stream disbursement toward air-defense replenishment and toward longer-range strike capabilities. The official noted that the disbursement would substantially augment the air-defense surge currently being executed under the Ramstein contact group framework.

The Russian Foreign Ministry's Wednesday-afternoon Moscow statement, delivered by spokesperson Maria Zakharova, characterized the imminent Council action as "a flagrant violation of established international law on sovereign-asset immunity" and indicated that Russia would pursue "all available legal and diplomatic countermeasures" against the framework's implementation. The Russian statement preserved the consistent diplomatic posture Moscow has maintained on the framework's substantive direction.

A senior European External Action Service official, asked Wednesday afternoon about the Russian response, said the Russian posture had been "fully anticipated" through the diplomatic process and that the European response to any Russian countermeasures was "carefully prepared" through extensive intra-bloc coordination during the past three months. The official noted that the European response framework was specifically calibrated to preserve the windfall framework's operational integrity against any Russian attempts to disrupt it.

A senior Commission official, in a Wednesday-evening Brussels briefing, said the substantive operational implementation of the framework following Tuesday's adoption would be "the most consequential phase" of the framework's development. The official said the Commission's operational team had been augmented during the past three weeks to handle the implementation cycle and that the European Investment Bank's administrative capacity for the trust mechanism had been similarly prepared.

The May 27 Council vote will be the principal European institutional event of the week, with the framework's adoption setting the foundation for the next stage of EU-Ukrainian engagement and providing the financial complement to the diplomatic and military support mechanisms that have characterized the bloc's response to the broader Russia-Ukraine war.

A senior NATO official, contacted Wednesday afternoon, said the NATO Secretariat had been "informally briefed" on the framework's substantive content and that the framework's operational implementation would be coordinated with NATO's parallel Ukrainian support mechanisms. The official said the substantive coordination had been a focus of bilateral consultations between the Commission and NATO during the past month.

The next major related event in the European calendar is the European Council heads-of-state summit on June 26-27 in Brussels, with the summit's substantive agenda including a review of the windfall framework's first operational cycle, the broader EU-Ukrainian strategic dialogue, and the next stage of the EU candidate-country accession framework.
