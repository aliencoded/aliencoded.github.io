US homeland security agencies have tightened domestic protective postures across transportation hubs, critical infrastructure sites, and large public events as the Iran conflict enters its second full week, officials said Monday.

The adjustments include increased visible patrols at airports and rail stations, expanded coordination between federal and state authorities, and additional advisories to operators of energy, water, and communications infrastructure. Officials emphasized that no specific credible threat has been disclosed publicly but said the elevated posture reflects standard practice during periods of heightened international tension.

Federal advisories sent to state and local law enforcement encouraged agencies to maintain vigilance against lone-actor threats, hate-motivated incidents targeting communities perceived as connected to the conflict, and exploitation of the news environment by unrelated criminal actors. Officials urged residents to report suspicious activity through existing channels.

Civil liberties organizations have raised concerns about potential overreach in the application of elevated postures, citing the experience of past crises. Officials have stressed that current measures are calibrated to specific risk indicators and do not include any broad expansion of surveillance authorities.

Faith and community organizations representing populations that have historically faced harassment during similar periods have coordinated with local law enforcement on protective measures. Several have reported increased outreach from federal liaison officers offering threat assessments and security guidance.

Transportation operators, particularly in aviation and rail, have implemented additional procedures in coordination with federal agencies. Officials say the changes are intended to be visible enough to deter while minimizing disruption to ordinary travel.

Health and emergency management agencies have reviewed continuity plans, including responses to potential cyber-physical incidents. The reviews are described as routine but with greater urgency given the broader threat environment.

Officials say the elevated posture is expected to remain in place as long as the underlying tensions persist. Agencies are preparing to maintain the current footing for an extended period rather than the days-to-weeks horizon often associated with discrete event-driven adjustments.
